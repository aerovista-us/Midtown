Lot 2 placement ideas

Three fast placement studies for the irregular 7,028 sf Lot 2 parcel, with Pennsylvania Avenue as the street frontage and an approximately 1,800 sf two-story home plus two-car garage as the working program.

Fast read

Use the depth. Keep the garage from owning the street.

The irregular south boundary should help shape the plan instead of being treated like a rectangular lot.

Working baseline: develop Option B first, then borrow Option A's stronger street presence and Option C's courtyard character where they improve the final plan.
A

Side Drive + Rear Garage

Clean street face; garage pushed deep into the lot.
Best: premium feel + Pennsylvania street presence
Why it works

Strongest Pennsylvania-facing house presence, a clear 10-foot side drive, and a meaningful rear yard zone.

Strength

Best curb appeal without a front garage door.

Watch-out

Rear / detached garage can add cost and needs fire and access review.

B

L-Shaped House

Footprint bends with the irregular south boundary.
Best: balanced cost + livability
Why it works

Integrated garage, protected patio, and a footprint that uses the angled lot line instead of fighting it.

Strength

Most efficient way to tuck the garage and patio.

Watch-out

Window placement and side-drive character need care so the narrow edge does not feel like an alley.

C

Front Courtyard + Side Garage

Courtyard entry with side-loaded garage mass.
Best: boutique / high-design product
Why it works

The courtyard makes the narrow frontage feel intentional, softens the garage from the street, and creates a strong arrival sequence.

Strength

Most architectural and distinctive of the three.

Watch-out

Drive and garage maneuvering should be proven with real vehicle turning templates early.

Quick comparison

Concept-level direction, not a code or engineering determination.

A

Highest street value

Strongest curb appeal and house-forward frontage; likely more paving and garage complexity.

B

Best working baseline

Strongest balance of cost, layout, patio, garage integration, and response to the lot geometry.

C

Most distinctive

Best design character and arrival experience; turning geometry needs proof before commitment.

Recommended next move: advance Option B as the first real schematic plan. Keep Option A's Pennsylvania-facing street-presence strategy, then test whether a smaller version of Option C's courtyard can be added without compromising driveway and garage geometry.
Conceptual massing only. Verify setbacks, access width, fire separation, utility constraints, turning geometry, and final building envelope before design development.
